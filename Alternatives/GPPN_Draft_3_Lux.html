This is Ephantus Kanyugi from Nairobi. For years he labelled data so that AI could learn. He told us: “Some of my co-workers had suicidal thoughts.”

Before AI can learn, people label and filter its data by hand. In the DLA’s own survey, six in ten call the work mentally exhausting. Our proposal, Homo post Machina, uses Europe’s market power to change that.

In 2022, OpenAI paid its Kenyan vendor 12 dollars 50 an hour. The workers took home between 1.32 and 2 dollars.

In 2026, labellers in the DLA’s survey still earn about 1 dollar 30 an hour. That is our estimate. Four years on, nothing has changed.

We know this because we work with the Data Labelers Association and their pay survey.

Kenya can’t do this alone: when Scale AI’s platform Remotasks left Kenya in 2024, it happened almost overnight. But the AI built on this work is sold here too: 450 million consumers, one rulebook.

This is the Brussels Effect. Whoever wants to sell in the EU follows EU rules, and many companies then apply them worldwide. The EU can use its market power to set standards that travel.

Backup: we borrow two existing EU tools, the energy label and the ecodesign rules.

Overview. We propose one policy for the European Commission, run by a taskforce in DG EMPL with the AI Office, and co-designed with the Data Labelers Association. It has two instruments.

Click 1. Instrument one makes the work visible: a campaign told by the workers themselves, a public accountability database, and a Fair AI label, like the EU energy label.

Click 2. Instrument two makes fair work a condition. A labour standard that builds on the DLA’s own EU-funded Code of Conduct. First in public AI contracts, then, as a proposal, for the whole EU market. And the work cannot just move away: once extended to the EU market, the rule covers AI sold here, wherever its data was labelled.

Backup, cost objection: doubling workers’ pay raises the hourly contract cost by roughly 11–16%, other costs held constant (own calculation from Perrigo 2023). OpenAI’s compute spend in 2024 was an estimated US$5 billion (Epoch AI 2025).

Overview. We pilot first, then scale, and it corrects itself every year: worker reports, audits, updated ratings.

Click 1. Year one: the Commission and the DLA set up the taskforce and co-design the standard.

Click 2. Years two and three: fair-work clauses in the EU institutions’ AI tenders, and the voluntary label.

Click 3. Year four: an independent evaluation decides. If it works, we extend to the EU market. If results are mixed, we simplify and extend the pilot. If vendors resist, we name them publicly and redesign.

Click 4. Only then: proposed minimum requirements for the whole EU market, decided by Parliament and Council.
