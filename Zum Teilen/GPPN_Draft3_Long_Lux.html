Kenya is one of many. Between 154 and 435 million people do online gig work, up to 12.5% of the world’s labour force. The hubs: Kenya, Venezuela, India, the Philippines.

And Kenya cannot solve this alone. Platforms can simply leave: Scale AI’s platform Remotasks left Kenya in 2024, almost overnight. That is why change has to come from the demand side.

Backup: the 154–435 million is all online gig work (World Bank), not only data labelling.

This is the Brussels Effect. Whoever wants to sell in the EU follows EU rules, and many companies then apply them worldwide. So the EU can use its market power to set standards that travel.

We borrow two tools the EU already uses: the energy label becomes a Fair AI label, and the ecodesign rules become a minimum labour standard.

Backup: especially likely for AI, because models are trained once and sold everywhere (Bradford 2020).

Overview. We propose one policy for the European Commission, run by a taskforce in DG EMPL with the AI Office, and co-designed with the Data Labelers Association. It has two instruments.

Click 1. Instrument one makes the work visible: a campaign told by the workers themselves, a public accountability database, and a Fair AI label, like the EU energy label.

Click 2. Instrument two makes fair work a condition. A labour standard that builds on the DLA’s own EU-funded Code of Conduct. First in public AI contracts, then, as a proposal, for the whole EU market.

Backup: procurement starts with the EU institutions’ own AI tenders; Member States are encouraged to follow. Costs and relocation are on the Objections slide.

Overview. We pilot first, then scale, and it corrects itself every year: worker reports, audits, updated ratings.

Click 1. Year one: the Commission and the DLA set up the taskforce and co-design the standard.

Click 2. Years two and three: fair-work clauses in the EU institutions’ AI tenders, and the voluntary label.

Click 3. Year four: an independent evaluation decides. If it works, we extend to the EU market. If results are mixed, we simplify and extend the pilot. If vendors resist, we name them publicly and redesign.

Click 4. Only then: proposed minimum requirements for the whole EU market, decided by Parliament and Council.

Two objections we expect. First: this makes AI in Europe too expensive. If workers’ pay doubles, the hourly contract cost rises by roughly 11 to 16%, with other costs held constant. Compare that with OpenAI’s estimated 5 billion dollars of compute spend in 2024.

Second: the work will just move somewhere else. Once extended to the EU market, the rule covers every AI product sold here, wherever its data was labelled.

Backup: the pilot itself only covers public contracts, which is why relocation is one of the three things the year-4 evaluation measures. Better pay and clear rules also improve annotation quality (Laux et al. 2023).
